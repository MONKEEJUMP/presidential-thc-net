## Primary-source verification

### Distillate

- Wang, L. et al., “Refining Cannabidiol Using Wiped-Film Molecular Distillation: Experimentation, Process Modeling, and Prediction,” *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research* (2022): https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.2c00290
  - Claim coverage: experimental use of wiped-film molecular distillation on hemp extract; stripping and refining as separate stages; supports the description of a thin-film feed and staged recovery of a cannabinoid-rich fraction.
- Sagili, S. U. K. R. et al., “Optimization of wiped-film short path molecular distillation for recovery of cannabinoids from cannabis oil using response surface methodology,” *Industrial Crops and Products* 195 (2023), 116442: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2023.116442
  - Claim coverage: experimental distillation of crude cannabis oil; two-cut separation in which terpenes and cannabinoids are associated with the first and second cuts; feed rate and condenser temperature as process variables; supports the explanation that process settings affect fraction recovery.

### Live resin and postharvest volatile retention

- Birenboim, M. et al., “In Pursuit of Optimal Quality: Cultivar-Specific Drying Approaches for Medicinal Cannabis,” *Plants* 13 (2024), 1049: https://doi.org/10.3390/plants13071049
  - Claim coverage: controlled experiment showing that drying conditions affect terpene preservation and that the result is cultivar-dependent; supports the copy's caution that fresh-frozen and cured material can present different volatile profiles without making one a universal quality winner.
- Spadafora, N. D. et al., “The influence of drying and storage conditions on the volatilome and cannabinoid content of Cannabis sativa L. inflorescences,” *Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry* 416 (2024), 3797–3809: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00216-024-05321-w
  - Claim coverage: primary GC×GC-MS and HPLC comparison of drying and storage conditions; supports the statements that postharvest handling changes volatile and cannabinoid profiles and that preservation is condition-dependent.

### Live rosin and freeze-drying

- Addo, P. W. et al., “Freeze-drying Cannabis sativa L. using real-time relative humidity monitoring and mathematical modeling for the cannabis industry,” *Industrial Crops and Products* 199 (2023), 116754: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2023.116754
  - Claim coverage: experimental cannabis freeze-drying work measuring drying kinetics, trichome structure, cannabinoids, and terpenes under different pre-freezing and drying conditions; supports the description of freeze-drying as a controlled material-removal stage rather than an inert pause.

The exact wash-bag stack, trichome range, premium fractions, 18–24 hour freeze-dry window, press-bag sizes, and press-temperature ranges come from the supplied Phase 6 record. No unsupported yield percentage was introduced.

### Liquid diamonds and THCa crystallization

- Dibble, C. J. and Cole, I. B., US Patent Application US20200102283A1, “Methods for Obtaining Purified Cannabis Extracts and THCA Crystals”: https://patents.google.com/patent/US20200102283A1/en
  - Claim coverage: inventor-filed process disclosure and examples for selectively crystallizing THCa from a cannabis solvent extract; reported crystal purity above 95% and a residual filtrate enriched in other cannabinoids and terpenes. This is a primary process disclosure, not a peer-reviewed comparison study.
- WO Patent Application WO2024076811A1, “Thin film oxidation of crystallized cannabis products”: https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024076811A1/en
  - Claim coverage: inventor-filed description of “diamond mining” as THCa crystallization from cannabinoid- and terpene-rich oil, followed by separation of the viscous high-terpene fraction from the crystals. This source supports terminology and process description only.

The 99%+ THCa ceiling, 358.47 and 314.46 g/mol molecular weights, 0.877 conversion factor, multi-week sealed-vessel hold, controlled recombination, and above-90% total-cannabinoid finish come from Phase 6. The cartridge discussion is limited to the physical need for a uniform, flowing oil; it makes no performance or effects claim.
